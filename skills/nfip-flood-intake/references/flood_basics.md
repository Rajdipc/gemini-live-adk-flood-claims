# What the NFIP means by "flood"

General guidance, paraphrased from the FEMA Standard Flood Insurance Policy
(SFIP) Dwelling Form. The policy wording itself is the authority.

## The definition, in plain language

A flood is a general and temporary condition where normally dry land is
partly or completely covered by water, and it affects either **two or more
acres** of normally dry land or **two or more properties** (at least one of
which is the policyholder's). The water must come from one of these:

- rivers, lakes, streams or the sea overflowing their banks (inland or tidal waters),
- an unusual and rapid build-up or run-off of surface water from any source, such as very heavy rain,
- mudflow (a river of liquid, flowing mud),
- collapse or erosion of land along a shore or lake caused by waves or currents above normal levels.

## What this means for intake

- The water came from **outside** and was **widespread**. Neighbours or the
  street flooded too. That points to flood.
- Water that started **inside** the building (a pipe, an appliance, a water
  heater), or that only affected this one home, is usually **not** a flood.
- Rain coming through a damaged roof or window is wind or rain damage, not
  flood, even during a hurricane.
- Storm surge flooding a coastal home is flood.

## Two properties or two acres: what to ask

"Did water also get into neighbouring homes or cover the street or yards
around you?" A yes supports the flood definition. A no does not end the claim;
record the answer and let the adjuster decide.

## Things NFIP policies generally limit or exclude

- **Basements** (a floor below ground level on all sides): coverage is
  generally limited to essential items such as furnaces, water heaters,
  electrical panels, and clean-up. Finished walls, carpet and most personal
  belongings in a basement are generally not covered.
- **Damage caused by moisture, mildew or mold** that the policyholder could
  have prevented after the flood.
- **Seepage** of groundwater through walls or floors without a general flood.
- **Sewer or drain backup**, unless a general flood in the area directly caused it.
- **Cars and vehicles**, money, and property outside the building such as fences, decks and landscaping.
- **Additional living expenses**, such as a hotel. An NFIP policy generally
  does not pay these.
