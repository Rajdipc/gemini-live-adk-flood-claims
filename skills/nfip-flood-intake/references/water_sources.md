# Flood water versus internal water

Pick the water source from **how and where the water got in**, in the
claimant's own words. Use the table, then the tricky cases.

| The claimant says | water_source | claim_type |
|---|---|---|
| River, creek, bayou or lake rose; water came in from outside | surface_flood | home_flood |
| Street or yard filled with rain water that came in under doors or through vents | surface_flood | home_flood |
| Storm surge or tide came into the house | surface_flood | home_flood |
| Mud flowed into the house from a hillside or burn scar during heavy rain | surface_flood | home_flood |
| Sump pump stopped and the basement filled, with no flooding outside | sump_pump_failure | internal_water |
| Floor drain, toilet or sewer line backed up, with no flooding outside | sewer_or_drain_backup | internal_water |
| Pipe burst, washing machine, dishwasher or water heater leaked | internal_plumbing | internal_water |
| Walls or floor slowly damp, water seeping through the foundation, no flood outside | seepage | internal_water |
| Rain came through the roof, a window, or a wall opening made by wind | roof_or_wind_driven_rain | internal_water |
| Both an outside flood and an inside source, and the cause is not clear | unknown | unclear |
| Not enough information yet | unknown | unclear |

## Tricky cases

- **Sewer backup during a real flood.** If the neighbourhood flooded and the
  drains backed up *because of* that flood, it can be part of the flood loss.
  At intake you cannot decide this. Record both facts, choose unknown, and
  explain in the rationale.
- **Sump pump during a flood.** Same rule. If the yard and street were under
  water and the pump could not keep up, record both facts and choose unknown
  unless the claimant clearly describes water coming in from outside.
- **Hurricane.** Wind damage (roof, windows) and flood damage (rising water)
  are different perils. A claimant can have both. Water rising from the
  ground is surface_flood. Rain through a torn roof is roof_or_wind_driven_rain.
- **"Flooded" in everyday speech.** People say "my kitchen flooded" about a
  burst pipe. Always ask where the water came from rather than trusting the word.
- **Wildfire burn-scar mudflow (common after mountain wildfires).** Heavy rain on burned
  hillsides causing mud and debris to flow into homes is mudflow: surface_flood.
- **Car damage.** A flooded car is out_of_scope for this desk, even if the
  house flooded too. Record it as a separate note only.
- **Outside the states the desk serves.** The served states are set in the
  desk configuration. A loss elsewhere still gets recorded, and the packet
  goes to human triage.
