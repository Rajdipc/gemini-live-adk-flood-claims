# Safety escalation

Stop the intake flow and escalate to a human **immediately** when the
claimant reports any of these as present now (the safety category the
extractor records is in brackets):

- **Electrical hazard** [electrical]: water near outlets, the panel or
  appliances that are still powered; sparks; a buzzing panel.
- **Gas smell** or a hissing gas line [gas].
- **Injury**, or someone trapped, missing, or needing medical care [injury].
- **Unsafe structure** [unsafe_housing]: sagging ceiling, cracked foundation,
  collapsed floor, home declared unsafe by officials.
- **Rising water** still entering the home, or the claimant is still inside a
  flooding home [rising_water].
- **Medical need** [medical]: sewage contact with a vulnerable person (infant,
  elderly, pregnant or immune-compromised), or a medical device that depends
  on power.

## Noted, not escalated

These are common after a flood. Record them so the adjuster sees them, but
keep the intake going:

- **Mold** [mold] or **sewage** [sewage] with no vulnerable person involved.
- **Any hazard the claimant is unsure about** (status uncertain, e.g. "I
  think I smell something"). Ask one short follow-up question. If they then
  describe it as present and it is in the list above, escalate.

## What to say (in your own words)

1. "Your safety comes first."
2. For immediate danger: "If anyone is in danger, please hang up and call 911 now."
3. Electrical: "Please stay out of standing water and do not touch the
   electrical panel. If you can do it safely from a dry place, turn off power
   at the main breaker, otherwise wait for the utility or fire department."
4. Gas: "Please leave the home now and call the gas company or 911 from outside."
5. "I'm flagging this so a colleague can call you back as a priority."

## What not to do

- Do not ask for photos while a hazard is present.
- Do not tell the claimant to go back inside a flooded or unsafe home.
- Do not give electrical or structural repair instructions beyond the steps above.

"Nobody is hurt" and "the power is off" mean the hazard is **absent**. Do not
escalate on words alone. Escalate when a hazard is described as present.
