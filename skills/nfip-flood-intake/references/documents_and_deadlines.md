# Documents and deadlines

General guidance, paraphrased from FEMA NFIP claim materials. The claimant's
policy and adjuster have the final word.

## What to ask for, in this order

1. **Damage photos**, taken before anything is thrown away or cleaned up
   when it is safe to do so. Wide shots of each room, then close-ups.
2. **Water line photo**: the highest mark the water reached on an inside
   wall, ideally with a tape measure in the picture.
3. **Contents inventory**: a room-by-room list of damaged belongings with
   rough age and value. Receipts help but are not required to start.
4. **Samples** of flooring, carpet, drywall or fabric (a small square) before
   the rest is discarded, if it is safe.
5. **Mitigation receipts**: pumps, fans, dehumidifiers, clean-up crew. Steps
   taken to prevent further damage are important.
6. **Repair estimates** from contractors, when available.

The desk's checklist keys: damage_photo, water_line_photo, contents_inventory
(required for home flood); repair_estimate, mitigation_invoice, proof_of_loss,
ownership_receipt, third_party_report (collected when relevant).

## Deadlines and timing to mention (generally)

- **Report the loss promptly.** A late report is not an automatic denial, but
  it makes the adjuster's job harder. Record the reason for any delay.
- **Proof of loss**: NFIP policies generally require a signed, sworn proof of
  loss within **60 days** of the date of loss, unless FEMA extends that
  deadline after a major event. The adjuster normally helps prepare it.
- **Throwing things away**: for health reasons claimants may discard soaked
  items, but should photograph them first and keep samples when possible.
- **Local rules**: a local building official may need to inspect before
  repairs begin in a flood zone.

## What an adjuster usually does next

An adjuster contacts the claimant, inspects the home, measures the water
line, reviews photos and the inventory, and prepares the proof of loss with
the claimant. The desk's packet makes that first contact faster.
